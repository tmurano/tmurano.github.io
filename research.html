Research

1. Activity-dependent control of neuronal maturation state

Granule cells of the hippocampal dentate gyrus can shift toward an immature-like state in adulthood. We found transcriptomic “immaturity” in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex of patients with alcoholism (Sci Rep 2017), and showed that the immaturity induced by neuronal hyperexcitation is shared by multiple neuropsychiatric disorders (Commun Biol 2019). Using repeated optogenetic stimulation of the dentate gyrus (REPOPS), we then showed that repetitive neuronal activation regulates the maturation state of neurons via nuclear reprogramming (Nat Commun 2026).

2. Mechanisms of antidepressant treatments

Electroconvulsive therapy strongly activates the dentate gyrus and has a striking therapeutic effect. Starting from this observation, we study the molecular and cellular basis of the antidepressant effects of repeated neuronal hyperactivity, using single-nucleus transcriptomics and epigenomics. This work is supported by JST FOREST and JSPS KAKENHI.

3. Computational tools for interpreting transcriptomes

We develop methods that score gene expression against external reference signatures, so that the state of a sample or a single cell can be read along biologically meaningful axes. RFind-sc ranks each cell by its deviation from expected counts and maps it, with a direction-aware Running Fisher test, onto axes defined by up- and down-regulated signatures (bioRxiv 2026; see Software).

Also: population coding in the dentate gyrus

With calcium imaging in freely moving mice and machine learning, we showed that multiple types of navigational information — position, speed, and direction of motion — are independently encoded in the population activity of dentate gyrus neurons (PNAS 2022).
