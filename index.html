Tomoyuki Murano

村野 友幸 · MD, PhD

I am a Lecturer in the Division of Systems Medical Science, Center for Medical Science, Fujita Health University, Japan, where I work in the Miyakawa Lab. My main research theme is neuronal hyperactivity, which I study from both sides: its pathological effects — how it contributes to psychiatric disorders — and its physiological effects — how repeated neuronal activation, as in electroconvulsive therapy, rewrites the state of neurons and produces antidepressant effects. I combine optogenetics, calcium imaging, transcriptomics and epigenomics, and meta-analysis of human disease data with computational tools that I develop.

Keywords: Neuronal hyperactivity · Electroconvulsive therapy and antidepressant mechanisms · Pathophysiology of psychiatric disorders · Transcriptome analysis and meta-analysis of human disorders · Epigenomics · Optogenetics · Calcium imaging · Bioinformatics

What I aim for

Precisely because new technologies keep making larger-scale measurement and manipulation of the brain possible, I believe research should be guided by fundamental questions — first, what is essentially important, and then, what we want to know — and these questions should shape how we choose and use the tools. I want to treat the cell state of a neuron itself — not only its firing or its synapses — as a unit of plasticity. My research began with a question raised by the striking therapeutic effect of electroconvulsive therapy, and has grown into the principle that neuronal hyperactivity can rewrite the state of mature neurons. I will continue to focus on new types of plasticity to uncover the basic principles of the brain and how they relate to disease.
