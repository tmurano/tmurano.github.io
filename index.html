About

I am a neuroscientist studying the hippocampal dentate gyrus. My work asks how the maturation state of neurons is controlled by neuronal activity, how neuronal hyperactivation produces the “immature dentate gyrus” phenotype shared across neuropsychiatric disorders, and how antidepressant treatments such as electroconvulsive therapy act on the brain. I combine optogenetics, calcium imaging, bulk and single-nucleus transcriptomics and epigenomics with computational tools that I develop for interpreting gene expression against reference signatures.

Fields: neuroscience, biological psychiatry, bioinformatics.

What I aim for

New technologies let us measure and manipulate the brain at unprecedented scale, but the questions should still drive the choice of tools. I want to treat the cell state of a neuron itself — not only its firing or its synapses — as a unit of plasticity. My research began with a question raised by the striking therapeutic effect of electroconvulsive therapy, and has grown into the principle that neuronal hyperactivity can rewrite the state of mature neurons. I hope to keep moving between questions about the mind and questions in neuroscience, and to open new research areas from there.
