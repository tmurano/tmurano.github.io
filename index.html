Tomoyuki Murano

村野 友幸 · MD, PhD

I am a Lecturer in the Division of Systems Medical Science, Center for Medical Science, Fujita Health University, Japan, where I work in the Miyakawa Lab. My main research theme is neuronal hyperactivity, which I study from both sides: its pathological effects — how it contributes to psychiatric disorders — and its physiological effects — how repeated neuronal activation, as in electroconvulsive therapy, rewrites the state of neurons and produces antidepressant effects. I combine optogenetics, calcium imaging, transcriptomics and epigenomics, and meta-analysis of human disease data with computational tools that I develop.

I received my MD from the University of Tokyo and my PhD from SOKENDAI (National Institute for Physiological Sciences). Fields: neuroscience, biological psychiatry, bioinformatics, calcium imaging.

What I aim for

Precisely because new technologies keep making larger-scale measurement and manipulation of the brain possible, I believe research should be guided by fundamental questions — first, what is essentially important, and then, what we want to know — and these questions should shape how we choose and use the tools. I want to treat the cell state of a neuron itself — not only its firing or its synapses — as a unit of plasticity. My research began with a question raised by the striking therapeutic effect of electroconvulsive therapy, and has grown into the principle that neuronal hyperactivity can rewrite the state of mature neurons. My life theme is to focus on new types of plasticity and to uncover both the basic principles of the brain and their links to disease.

The gene expression patterns as surrogate indices of pH in the brain Hideo Hagihara, Tomoyuki Murano, Tsuyoshi Miyakawa Frontiers in Psychiatry 14, 1151480 (2023) paperPDF

A meta-analysis of 281 human datasets from 11 CNS disorders showing that gene expression associated with lower brain pH is over-represented in disorders such as schizophrenia, bipolar disorder and autism.

Multiple types of navigational information are independently encoded in the population activities of the dentate gyrus neurons Tomoyuki Murano, Ryuichi Nakajima, Akito Nakao, Nao Hirata, Satoko Amemori, Akira Murakami, Yukiyasu Kamitani, Jun Yamamoto, Tsuyoshi Miyakawa PNAS 119(32), e2106830119 (2022) paperPDFdata

A study that used calcium imaging and machine learning in freely moving mice to reveal that position, speed and motion direction are independently encoded in populations of dentate gyrus neurons.

Expression of progenitor cell/immature neuron markers does not present definitive evidence for adult neurogenesis Hideo Hagihara, Tomoyuki Murano, Koji Ohira, Miki Miwa, Katsuki Nakamura, Tsuyoshi Miyakawa Molecular Brain 12, 108 (2019) paperPDF

A review arguing that, because mature neurons can “dematurate” and re-express immature-neuron markers, these markers alone are not definitive evidence of adult neurogenesis in humans.

Transcriptomic immaturity inducible by neural hyperexcitation is shared by multiple neuropsychiatric disorders Tomoyuki Murano, Hideo Hagihara, Katsunori Tajinda, Mitsuyuki Matsumoto, Tsuyoshi Miyakawa Communications Biology 2, 32 (2019) paperPDFexplainer (Japanese)

A study showing, across 87 disease datasets, that transcriptomic pseudo-immaturity induced by neuronal hyperexcitation is shared by multiple neuropsychiatric disorders, including schizophrenia, Alzheimer's disease and ALS.

Transcriptomic immaturity of the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex in patients with alcoholism Tomoyuki Murano, Hisatsugu Koshimizu, Hideo Hagihara, Tsuyoshi Miyakawa Scientific Reports 7, 44531 (2017) paperPDF

A study showing immature-like gene expression in the post-mortem hippocampus and prefrontal cortex of patients with alcoholism, with a dominant contribution of fast-spiking neurons.
